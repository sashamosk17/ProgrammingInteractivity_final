How JPEG Compression Works

A step-by-step visualization of color space, spatial frequencies, and quantization — the stage that makes the file smaller.

Introduction

JPEG is a lossy format for continuous-tone images. It does not remove random pixels: instead, the image is converted into a form that better matches the limits of human vision.

This article follows the key stages of the algorithm using one test frame. Change the quality setting and watch the quantization tables, artifacts, and numerical metrics change together.

How to read the visualization. The table values are divisors for DCT coefficients. The larger the divisor, the more coarsely the corresponding frequency is rounded.

Discrete cosine transform

Each channel is divided into 8×8 blocks. The DCT replaces 64 pixel values with 64 coefficients. The upper-left coefficient describes the average level; moving right and down increases spatial frequency.

Preset
$$ F(u,v) = \frac{1}{4} C(u)C(v) \sum_{x=0}^{7} \sum_{y=0}^{7} f(x,y) \cos\left(\frac{(2x+1)u\pi}{16}\right) \cos\left(\frac{(2y+1)v\pi}{16}\right) $$

F(u,v) is a weighted sum of pixels. The transform is reversible; information is discarded later, during quantization.

Quantization

Quantization divides each DCT coefficient by a table value and rounds the result. At low quality, divisors become larger, many coefficients become zero, and fine detail disappears.

$$ \hat{F}(u,v) = \mathrm{round}\left( \frac{F(u,v)}{Q(u,v)} \right) \quad \text{and} \quad \hat{F}_{\text{reconstructed}}(u,v) = \hat{F}(u,v) \cdot Q(u,v) $$
Preset

Higher values preserve more high-frequency detail.

Results

Metrics are recalculated whenever the quality or preset changes. PSNR is higher and MSE is lower when the reconstructed image is closer to the original.
